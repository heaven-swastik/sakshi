Slide 2 · Proposed Solution

The Regulatory Crisis vs. SAKSHI's Three Locks

THE PRIVACY & REGULATORY DILEMMA

1. Supreme Court July 2026 Ruling:

Supreme Court ordered pan-India consent forms must allow students to OPT OUT of Aadhaar-linked APAAR. Prior systems penalise opt-outs as fraudulent.

2. Up to ₹250 Cr DPDP Act Penalties:

Centralising raw APAAR & Aadhaar creates a catastrophic regulatory honeypot. A single leak invokes heavy statutory fines on the regulator.

3. Seat Blocking & Ghost Seats:

Colleges submit phantom rolls to satisfy AICTE's 30% intake viability rule, while students hold multiple seats across universities.

SAKSHI'S SOLUTION: THE THREE CRYPTOGRAPHIC LOCKS

✔ Lock 1: Ephemeral Ingestion & Salted Blind Tokenizer

Colleges upload CSVs; APAAR IDs are immediately converted into salted HMAC-SHA256 tokens in volatile memory. Raw numbers are wiped within milliseconds. Zero PII ever touches database disk.

✔ Lock 2: Triangulated Seat Integrity Scoring (0–100)

7 deterministic mathematical rules (R1-R7) evaluate batch sizes, sanctioned intake, quota distribution, and cross-college duplicates. Full regulatory clarity without viewing names.

✔ Lock 3: Dual-Control Reveal & Citizen Transparency Window

AICTE officials cannot unseal student identities unilaterally. Deanonymization requires mutual cryptographic consent or direct student confirmation via the Citizen Portal.

Covenant: "A witness that confirms, never discloses."
